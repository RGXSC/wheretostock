Where to Stock

Running the money

Cash goes out to the tables while they sell, and comes back in three pieces once the season is over. What is left in front of a team at the end is its margin — the same five lines the screen shows, in the same order, done with paper first.

Setup — before week 1

  • Each table draws a card face down and types its two numbers into the setup screen: target sell-through, and how much of production goes to the boutiques at launch.
  • The cards guarantee the rule: no two tables play the same sell-through, no two the same push. Nothing to police.
  • Say what production means before they lock it in: 200 € leaves the company for every bag made, sold or not. They are not paying it yet. They will.

During the season — pay them as they sell

After each week's Sell step, pay the table 1 000 € for every bag it sold that week. The screen has the number.

The reckoning — after week 13

The table is holding its turnover. Take it back in this order, out loud, and let them count it out themselves.

1

What you tooktheir pile

“That is your turnover. Every bag you sold.”
2

The fixed cost− 120 000 €

“The boutiques, the people, the campaign. Due whether you sold one bag or four hundred.”
3

The cost of what sold− 200 € × sold

“Making the bags that found a customer. Money well spent.”
4

The cost of what did not− 200 € × unsold

“And this is the pile you made for nobody. It is gone. It was gone the day you decided how many to produce.”

What is still on the table is the margin. Ask them to push it to the middle before you show the screen — the biggest pile in the room is the winner, and everyone can already see which it is.

The screen tells the same story, in the same order

The game's P&L and the debrief's bridge both read turnover → fixed cost → cost of what sold → cost of what did not → margin. The last deduction is the line the whole session is about, and by the time it appears on screen they have already handed those notes back across the table.

What to print

Before the room arrives: print and cut both PDFs, sort the notes into six banks — one per table — and keep the change (100 / 200 / 500) in a pile of your own: every payout is a multiple of 1 000 €, so the small notes only appear at the end, when the tables pay 200 € a bag. Sized for six tables playing to the end. Every note is on its own sheet pair — fronts on one side, backs on the other — so print double-sided and cut along the shared edges. There is nothing to trim between notes.

Print a denomination by selecting its two pages and asking for N copies, double-sided. Two guillotine passes: the vertical cut, then the horizontals. The 5 000 is twice the 100 in both dimensions — the piles sort themselves.

Twenty-nine of the forty-five sheets are 5 000 € notes

Six tables ending on 260 000 € each is 1.56 M€, and 5 000 is the biggest note in the set. A 10 000 € note would take the run under thirty; one line in the file.

Five sell-through levels and seven pushes exist, so five tables can be distinct on both axes and no more. Cards 6 and 7 keep a unique push and repeat a sell-through, and say so on the face. Hand out 1–5 first.

If a table asks whether the cards are fair

They are not meant to be. The five pairs are scattered on purpose: the tidy set, highest sell-through against lowest push, would build a correlation into the room's results that the game never put there. Five strategies get played at once and compared — nobody starts even.
